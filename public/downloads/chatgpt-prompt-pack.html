ChatGPT Prompt Pack

Copy a template, replace the words in [brackets], and paste into ChatGPT. Add context (audience, goal, length, tone) for better answers.

How to use any prompt

  • Give a role + task + constraints Who should ChatGPT be, what to produce, and what to avoid.
  • Paste source material Articles, offers, notes, or code go after the instruction.
  • Ask for a format Table, numbered list, ad copy, lesson plan, or 5 bullets.
  • Iterate once “Make it 50% shorter”, “more specific”, or “keep my voice”.

Core templates (from your list)

1. Act as a…

Use a specialist so the answer matches a job, not a generic chatbot.

Act as a [job / expert].
Your audience is [who].
Goal: [what I need].
Constraints: [tone, length, tools, things to avoid].

Task: [the actual request].
Act as a senior digital marketer.
Your audience is busy founders who hate jargon.
Goal: a 7-day content plan for LinkedIn.
Constraints: 3 posts max per week, no hashtag stuffing.

Task: plan posts that promote a free ChatGPT prompt pack.

2. From the perspective of…

Force a viewpoint: customer, competitor, student, or skeptic.

From the perspective of a [person / role],
review [product / idea / text].

Cover:
- what they care about
- objections
- what would make them say yes

Be honest. Do not flatter.

3. Explain it to a 4th grader

Simplifies jargon without dumbing down the idea.

Explain [topic] to a 4th grader.
Use short sentences, a simple analogy, and one example.
Then add a 3-bullet “adult version” with the real terms.

4. Turn it into a table

Great for plans, features, and study notes.

Turn this into a table with columns:
[Column A] | [Column B] | [Column C]

Rules: one idea per row, no fluff, fill every cell.

Content:
[paste notes]

5. Comparison list for a decision

Create a comparison list to help me choose between [option A] and [option B] for [goal].

Include:
- best for
- not ideal for
- cost / time
- risk
- 1 recommended pick and why

Keep it practical, not salesy.

6. Summarise all this and…

Summarise all of this and [next action: extract tasks / write an email / list decisions].

Format:
1) 5-sentence summary
2) key facts
3) open questions
4) recommended next step

7. Teach me as a complete beginner

Teach me [skill] as a complete beginner.
Assume I know nothing.
Use: what it is, why it matters, a tiny first exercise, common mistakes, and what to learn next.
Keep lessons under 8 minutes of reading.

8. Write a social media post for…

Write a social media post for [platform] about [topic].
Audience: [who].
Goal: [save / click / comment / DM].
Tone: [friendly / expert / witty].
Include: hook, value, CTA.
Also give 3 headline variants and 5 hashtags max.

9. What advice can you give?

What advice can you give about [situation]?
Context: [facts].
Constraints: [budget, time, skills].
Give: 3 options, trade-offs, and the first step I should take this week.

Ready-to-run examples

Facebook ad — 60-day yoga course for busy professionals

Create persuasive Facebook ad copy for a 60-day yoga online course targeted at busy professionals.

Offer: 60-day yoga online course, 20 minutes/day, live + recordings.
Audience: 28–45, desk jobs, stressed, little time.
Objections: “I don’t have time”, “I’m not flexible”, “I start and quit”.
Must include: primary text (125 words max), headline (40 chars), description (30 chars), 3 CTA options.
Tone: calm, credible, not spiritual cliché. No fake urgency.
Asset Draft you can steal and edit
Primary text You don’t need a 90-minute class. You need 20 focused minutes that survive a packed calendar. This 60-day online yoga course is built for professionals who sit all day: short sessions, replay if you miss live, and a plan that starts at beginner. Unwind after work, loosen a stiff back, and actually finish a program. Start day 1 this week — no studio, no commute.
Headline 20-min yoga. 60 days. Done.
Description For busy desks, not studios.

Teach me to become a great public speaker despite my fear of public speaking.
I freeze, rush, and forget my points.
Give:

Quality checklist

  • Did I name the audience? Busy professional, 4th grader, hiring manager, etc.
  • Did I name the output? Ad copy, table, 5-sentence list, 7-day plan.
  • Did I add limits? Length, tone, “don’t invent facts”, “don’t use clichés”.
  • Did I paste the raw material? Article, offer, or notes belong in the same message.
