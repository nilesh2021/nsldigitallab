UI/UX Portfolio Checklist

Review your portfolio section by section before you apply for internships, freelance projects, or UI/UX jobs. Tick each item only when it is actually done.

For UI/UX students, freshers, junior designers, designers changing careers, and experienced designers updating their portfolio.

Before You Apply

The most important final checks. If any of these fail, fix them first.

  • Every project link and prototype link works Open each one in a private browser window, logged out.
  • Resume, LinkedIn, and portfolio tell the same story Same title, dates, roles, and projects across all three.
  • Your best 2–4 projects match the role you are applying for Lead with the project most relevant to this specific job.
  • Each case study shows your process, not just final screens Problem, your role, research, decisions, iterations, and outcome.
  • Contact details are current and easy to find Email and LinkedIn visible from every page.
  • The portfolio works well on a phone Recruiters often open links on mobile first.
  • No spelling or grammar errors Run a spell checker, then ask someone else to read it.

1. Portfolio Basics

  • Add a clear professional title e.g. "UI/UX Designer" or "Product Designer", matching the roles you apply for.
  • Write a short introduction One or two lines on who you are and what kind of design you do.
  • Show your location and contact information City, country, and whether you are open to remote or relocation.
  • Link your LinkedIn profile Use your custom LinkedIn URL, not the default one with numbers.
  • Show a professional email address Preferably your name, and make it clickable.
  • Use a clean portfolio URL Your own domain or a short, readable link you can share anywhere.
  • Add a resume/CV link A clearly labelled link or button, visible without searching.

2. About Me

  • Write a clear introduction Human and specific. Avoid generic lines like "I am passionate about design".
  • Summarise relevant experience Jobs, internships, freelance work, or strong academic projects.
  • Mention your design interests e.g. SaaS, mobile apps, accessibility, design systems, fintech.
  • List your core skills Research, wireframing, UI design, prototyping, usability testing.
  • List the tools you use Only tools you can confidently use in an interview task, e.g. Figma.
  • State your professional goals The kind of role, team, or product you want to work on next.

3. Project Selection

  • Show 2–4 strong projects Fewer, deeper case studies beat many shallow ones.
  • Include different project types where appropriate e.g. a mobile app, a web product, and a redesign.
  • Choose projects relevant to the target role Reorder or swap projects for each type of job you apply for.
  • Clearly identify your personal contribution For team projects, say exactly what you did and what others did.
  • Remove unnecessary weak projects Your portfolio is judged by its weakest project too.

4. Case Study Structure

  • Project overview Product, platform, timeline, team, and your role in 3–4 lines.
  • Problem statement What was wrong, for whom, and why it mattered.
  • Goals User goals and business goals you designed for.
  • My role Your responsibilities and the decisions you owned.
  • Research Methods used (interviews, surveys, competitor analysis) and key findings.
  • User needs The main needs and pain points the research revealed.
  • Personas, where relevant Only if based on research. Skip decorative personas.
  • User journey The end-to-end experience, including pain points and opportunities.
  • User flow The key steps users take to complete the main task.
  • Wireframes Early structure and layout options, with notes on what changed.
  • UI design Final high-fidelity screens for the most important parts of the flow.
  • Prototype A working link or short video of the main interaction.
  • Testing How you tested, with how many users, and what you learned.
  • Iterations Before-and-after examples showing what changed after feedback.
  • Final solution How the final design solves the original problem.
  • Results Metrics, feedback, or outcomes. Be honest if the project is conceptual.
  • Learnings What you would do differently next time.

5. Visual Presentation

  • Use a strong project cover A clear image of the product plus the project name and one-line summary.
  • Create a clear hierarchy Headings, subheadings, and body text are easy to tell apart.
  • Keep typography consistent One or two typefaces, with the same sizes used for the same roles.
  • Keep spacing consistent Use a spacing scale so sections and images line up across pages.
  • Use high-quality UI screenshots Sharp, uncropped, and showing real content rather than lorem ipsum.
  • Use appropriate mockups Device mockups should support the screens, not distract from them.
  • Check image resolution Crisp on retina screens, but compressed so pages still load fast.
  • Make layouts easy to scan Short paragraphs, captions on images, and key points highlighted.
  • Avoid excessive decoration Remove effects, patterns, or animations that compete with your work.

6. UX Process

  • Explain your design decisions For each key screen, say why it looks and works the way it does.
  • Show research evidence Quotes, data, survey results, or observations, not just claims.
  • Explain important iterations What you tried, what failed, and what you changed.
  • Connect decisions to user problems Every major design choice links back to a need or pain point.
  • Avoid showing only final screens Hiring managers want to see how you think, not just the result.

7. UI Design

  • Typography A clear type scale with readable sizes and line heights.
  • Color system Defined primary, neutral, and semantic colors used consistently.
  • Spacing A consistent spacing scale (e.g. 4 or 8 point) across screens.
  • Grid and layout Screens follow a clear column grid with aligned elements.
  • Components Reusable buttons, inputs, and cards, including their states.
  • Responsive design Show how key screens adapt to mobile, tablet, and desktop.
  • Accessibility considerations Color contrast, touch target sizes, focus states, and readable text.
  • Design consistency The same patterns behave the same way throughout the product.

8. Project Outcomes

  • Share results where available e.g. task completion, conversion, time on task, or support tickets.
  • Include user feedback Short quotes from testing sessions or real users.
  • Show business impact where available Revenue, retention, or efficiency gains, if you can share them.
  • Explain what changed because of the design For users, the team, or the product roadmap.
  • Share lessons learned Honest reflection shows maturity and growth.

9. Portfolio UX

  • Navigation is easy Work, About, Resume, and Contact reachable in one click.
  • Project hierarchy is clear Your strongest and most relevant project appears first.
  • Mobile responsive Text, images, and navigation work on small screens.
  • Fast loading Compress large images and videos. Test on a slow connection.
  • All links work Navigation, social, resume, and external links.
  • Prototype links work Figma links are set to "anyone with the link can view".
  • Contacting you is easy A visible email, contact button, or form on every page.

10. Job Application Readiness

  • Resume matches your portfolio The same projects, roles, and skills appear in both.
  • LinkedIn matches your resume Job titles, dates, and headline are consistent.
  • Portfolio matches the target role Projects and wording reflect what the job description asks for.
  • Contact details are current Email, phone, location, and links are up to date everywhere.
  • No spelling or grammar errors Check the portfolio, resume, and LinkedIn.
  • All project links work Test again right before you send each application.
  • Resume is downloadable A PDF with a clear filename, e.g. Firstname-Lastname-UIUX-Resume.pdf.
  • Portfolio is ready to share No placeholder text, unfinished pages, or "coming soon" projects.

11. Final Review

  • UX process
  • UI quality
  • Visual consistency
  • Accessibility
  • Mobile responsiveness
  • Performance

  • Contact information
